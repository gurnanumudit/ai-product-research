# Research article writing and link conventions

Owner preference, October 31, 2026. Apply to new articles and future revisions; preserve frozen historical artifacts. Updating this guidance does not authorize website publication.

- Label the link to an article’s research implementation **Research code**. Keep the destination in GitHub, preferably pinned to the relevant commit. Use the same visible wording in the header, appendix and other articles; an external-link icon is optional. Do not vary it to “Research code on GitHub” or “Research source and tests on GitHub”.
- Prefer short sentences, commas and colons over em-dash interruptions. Revise sentence structure rather than replacing punctuation mechanically. Preserve mathematical minus signs and range notation.
- Introduce related research briefly where it informs the interpretation. Put paper links, findings, relevance and important differences in an appendix. Related findings are not direct replications unless they test the same claim.
- Every appendix section needs a descriptive contextual link from the main narrative and a working return link. A generic appendix contents link is not enough. Deep links should open the target disclosure automatically.
- Keep the story sequential: explain the workflow, motivate the different feedback conditions, then show outcomes. Avoid repeating sample sizes in large panels or tables unless they help explain a comparison.
